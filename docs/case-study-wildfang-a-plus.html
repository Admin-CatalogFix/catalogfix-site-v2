Case study · Wildfang

A+ Content restored in 8 European marketplaces.

For years, Wildfang couldn’t touch its A+ Content in most of Europe. Two agencies, countless support cases and several executive escalations later, nobody could say why. Here’s how we finally got it back.

The problem

A missing tool, in exactly the markets that needed it most.

A+ Content is the extra images and text further down a product page. It’s where a brand explains what makes its products different. In France, Italy, Spain, the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium, Poland and Sweden, Wildfang couldn’t open A+ Content Manager at all, so it couldn’t create or change A+ Content there.

The problem was already there under the brand’s previous agency. And it got more costly over time: we were writing new listings in French, Italian, Spanish, Dutch, Polish and Swedish, and launching the range in four new countries, while building Premium A+ Content for seven product lines in the US and Canada. Shoppers in those 8 markets still saw plain listings.

The long road

No button to press, so we didn’t let the case close.

  1. July 2025: we took over the account

    The A+ problem came with it, unsolved.

  2. Case after case

    We opened more Seller Support cases than we could count and spent many hours on them. None of them found the cause.

  3. Executive escalations

    We escalated to Amazon’s executive escalations team several times. Still no fix.

  4. 24 October 2026: the right people

    We kept reaching out until the case reached Amazon specialists who knew where to look. They found that the account had been removed from A+ Content access by mistake when Amazon’s Launchpad program ended, and restored it in all 8 marketplaces.

  5. Straight away: content live

    We published A+ Content in the restored marketplaces straight away, and let the Wildfang team know.

The result

A+ Content Manager back in every affected marketplace.

8 marketplaces

France, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, Sweden, the UK, Spain and Belgium. Wildfang can now add and update A+ Content in these markets, as it already could in the US and Canada.
